## Why this source matters

Stream 01 cited this study when arguing that putting an LLM inside an agent loop did not by itself solve healthcare-agent reliability or efficiency. It helped elevate agent architecture and evaluation as research problems.

## What the source actually establishes at screening depth

The official record evaluates LLM-based agent systems on clinical decision tasks and frames systematic real-world benchmarking as limited. The historical discussion used the reported performance/efficiency trade-offs and persistent hallucination concerns to question whether contemporary agentic architectures reliably outperform strong base models enough to justify their added complexity.

## Project interpretation

**Historical Stream 01 inference:** the study supported treating orchestration, reliability, and evaluation as unresolved problems rather than assuming agent wrappers are intrinsically superior.

## What this source does NOT establish

- It does not establish that all agent architectures provide only modest gains.
- It does not establish a universal token/latency trade-off across models and tasks.
- It does not establish which dissertation architecture would solve the observed limitations.
- It does not prove that trustworthy-agent research is unsaturated.
