# User Goals and Constraints

## Degree context

- PhD field: Health Informatics
- Institution: University of Minnesota
- Primary advisor: must be an eligible IHI faculty member
- External/complementary advisor: Dr. Rui Zhang

## Research-selection objectives

The dissertation research program should ideally:

1. Produce scientifically meaningful and novel research.
2. Support multiple strong publications rather than one monolithic project.
3. Increase the probability of producing independently cited work.
4. Support a strong specialized-visa research portfolio.
5. Develop skills resistant to near-term AI commoditization.
6. Develop skills likely to remain valuable in the US AI economy.
7. Provide substantial technical depth rather than merely using APIs.
8. Develop skills transferable outside healthcare.
9. Preserve the possibility of pivoting into fields such as general technology, AI research, quantitative research, scientific computing, and data/AI infrastructure.
10. Have meaningful commercialization or startup potential.
11. Be feasible within the remaining PhD timeline.
12. Have strong local advisor and infrastructure support.

## Research philosophy

Healthcare should not necessarily define the methodology.

A preferred structure may be:

**generalizable methodological contribution + healthcare as the primary evaluation/application domain**

rather than:

**healthcare-specific application + minor technical contribution**

## Additional strategic constraints

- Publication feasibility and publication velocity are first-class design constraints.
- Coherence is preferred over accumulating unrelated papers.
- Advisor availability should be treated primarily as a feasibility screen; once a viable team exists, small differences in advisor fit should not dominate topic ranking.
- Industry demand and commercialization evidence should inform strategy, but scientific novelty must be established from research literature.
