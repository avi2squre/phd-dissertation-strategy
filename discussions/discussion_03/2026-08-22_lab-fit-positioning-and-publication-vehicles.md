## Why this is archived

Between the cohort findings and the direction discussion, the stream produced positioning reasoning that bears on candidate selection but is not in either neighboring entry: where the user's work actually sits in the funded project's task structure, what the research group's center of gravity is, and the first concrete publication vehicle for the empirical work. ## Digest

**Source-supported (kickoff deck), after correction.**

1. **The user's role in the funded project.** The stream first claimed the cohort report "is literally the Q1 WP1 deliverable" and referred to a "Task 1.1 cohort construction"; on the user's challenge both claims were retracted as invented. The deck itself shows: data preparation (1.1) and annotation (1.2) are assigned to the French site; the user's tasks are phenotype extraction (1.3) and normalization (1.4), assigned to the US site, with 1.3 starting in year-1 Q3. Consequences drawn: the summer cohort work is ahead of schedule rather than on it, and the specificity finding gains value (extraction over the raw cohort would have processed mostly non-target patients).
2. **Two unresolved critical-path questions for the collaborator.** The deck's expected cohort counts substantially exceed what the warehouse yields for two of the three codes (magnitudes in the internal report), which affects the data plan and was flagged as the first thing to raise. And no task in the table assigns annotation or adjudication to the US site, while US extraction needs annotated data; the adjudicated-gold stratum of the cohort plan needs clinician time that is not the user's to schedule (see the feasibility entry).
3. **Positioning argument.** With international collaboration constrained (documented mentor guidance from July), Aim 1 is the part of the project that can proceed unilaterally; anchoring the user's role there is robust to collaboration risk.

**Inference (stream 03).**

4. **Lab center of gravity.** From six documented group-meeting updates: the group's recurring patterns are LLM and agent methods on biomedical tasks, clinical NLP over EHR notes, and large-scale cohort and data work; annotation is normal work in the group. Directions that sit inside this center rather than adjacent to it: evaluation and benchmarking of clinical AI agents (consistent with the mentor sharing an agent-evaluation benchmark, LIT-014, at the meeting), and rare-disease phenotyping with verification (the user's current work extended). A collision-and-collaboration flag was raised: another student in the group works on a rare-disease LLM-extraction project targeting the same venue deadline; talking to them before settling a direction was advised. The proposal rule stated: the strongest proposal sits inside the lab's center of gravity rather than adjacent to it.
5. **Fellowship framing.** The renewal letter's stated purpose is methodological innovation in informatics research; the stream advised positioning the dissertation contribution as the verification and extraction architecture, with cohort characterization as groundwork, and noted that the fellowship's determination of "active participation" belongs to an academic advisor who is not yet formalized (an unresolved administrative gap, carried to September).
6. **The ranked-proposals document plan.** The user proposed preparing ranked research proposals for the mentor and collaborator, ranked by his long-term criteria; the stream advised that a document going to faculty should rank on research grounds (novelty, feasibility with available data, fit with lab strengths, venue), with career criteria informing the ranking privately, and raised the one-document-or-two question. The user stated plainly that the career reasoning stays with him. This exchange is the direct ancestor of the two-layer structure later codified (DEC-006 and the private strategy layer). One legitimate bridge was noted: preferring directions that produce a measurable contribution within a year is a research argument, not a euphemism.
7. **Advisor-landscape reading, on public grounds.** A remark by the collaborator about a program faculty member's project-effort allocation was re-read as scoped to one grant and a limited window, not evidence about willingness to advise students; the stream flagged separately that if that path fails, the backup bench is thin on appointment-type grounds (one candidate is affiliate rather than core faculty; the advisor of record's fit is unestablished; one monitoring-committee member is an existing relationship). Recorded as an open September question, not a conclusion; `context/advisor_landscape.md` is not edited by this entry.

**Untested option (publication).**

8. **The specificity finding as a poster.** A student poster at the 2027 informatics summit (submission deadline 2026-09-03) was identified as the vehicle for the cohort work's generalizable finding: commonly used diagnosis codes retrieve substantially heterogeneous populations for rare-disease cohorts, and cohort validation against original clinical names is a necessary step before EHR-based phenotyping. Open at archiving: whether to submit, and the authorship question (the student-poster category restricts co-authors, and the collaborator defined the cohort).
9. **A precedent to examine (LIT-015).** A 2024 machine-learning pipeline for detecting the target disease family from EHRs, from the project's partner site, was discovered during reference work and flagged as direct precedent to read before any novelty claim in this area; unread at archiving.
