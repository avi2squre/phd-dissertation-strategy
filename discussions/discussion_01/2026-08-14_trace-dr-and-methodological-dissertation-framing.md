# 2026-08-14 — TRACE-DR and methodological dissertation framing

## Historical role

This entry reconstructs the first substantive dissertation-direction episode in the available Stream 01 source. It began as a request for a model Preliminary Written Examination (PWE) proposal and became an early dissertation-strategy exercise.

TRACE-DR was selected by Stream 01 as the topic of that exemplar in response to the user's requested strategic constraints. It was not selected by the user or accepted as the dissertation direction.

## User-stated requirements

The user asked for a technically ambitious Health Informatics topic that could support a strong doctoral portfolio, durable technical skills, transferability beyond healthcare, strong publication potential, local advisor complementarity, and meaningful translational value. These internal strategic requirements were broader than the criteria that would later be considered appropriate for faculty-facing discussion.

## Initial Stream 01 proposal

Stream 01 proposed **TRACE-DR: Traceable Reasoning and Causal Evidence for Drug Repurposing — A Provenance-Aware Agentic AI Framework Integrating Biomedical Knowledge Graphs and Longitudinal Real-World Data**.

The central proposed workflow was:

biomedical evidence
→ graph-grounded hypothesis
→ explicit causal question
→ target-trial specification
→ deterministic longitudinal EHR/RWD analysis
→ sensitivity analysis
→ calibrated conclusion with provenance

### Stream 01 strategic judgment

The intended contribution was deliberately framed as more than "an LLM for healthcare". The proposed technical spine combined:

- agentic tool orchestration;
- biomedical NLP and knowledge representation;
- knowledge graphs;
- causal inference and target-trial reasoning;
- longitudinal real-world data;
- provenance and auditability;
- database/statistical execution;
- rigorous evaluation.

The argument was that the scientific contribution should survive replacement of the underlying foundation model because the methodological object was the auditable reasoning-and-validation pipeline rather than a particular model or prompt.

## Dissertation-coherence concern raised in the episode

While explaining how a strong PWE should be defended, Stream 01 raised a consequential question about TRACE-DR itself:

> Is this one scientific thesis, or several sophisticated projects placed next to each other?

The discussion explicitly anticipated committee questions such as:

- Why is an agentic architecture necessary rather than a deterministic pipeline?
- How can failures of causal identification be separated from failures in model-generated target-trial specifications?
- If the biomedical KG already contains a relation, what is the system actually discovering?
- What single scientific thesis connects KG reasoning, causal inference, and agent evaluation?

### Later generalization from this episode

The August 14 discussion established the underlying coherence concern: a technically broad proposal still needs a defensible central scientific thesis connecting its aims. Later Stream 01 discussions generalized this into a candidate-design rule: technical breadth alone is insufficient; each dissertation program should be organized around one central methodological claim whose aims test different aspects of that claim.

## Transition from exemplar to dissertation methodology

After the exemplar was produced, the discussion shifted from the content of TRACE-DR itself toward the reusable reasoning process behind a defensible doctoral proposal:

research area
→ unresolved problem
→ literature gap
→ central hypothesis
→ specific aims
→ experiments
→ statistical tests
→ anticipated results
→ failure contingencies
→ dissertation contribution

This transition is why the PWE exercise became relevant to later dissertation-topic selection rather than remaining merely an exam-preparation artifact.

## Publication and artifact logic

The proposal was designed around multiple independently useful outputs rather than one monolithic system. The historical discussion emphasized:

- falsifiable hypotheses;
- strong baselines and ablations;
- negative controls and sensitivity analyses;
- temporal evaluation to reduce knowledge leakage;
- explicit failure modes and alternative strategies;
- reproducibility and auditability;
- human evaluation when appropriate;
- methodological contributions that could decompose into multiple papers.

This was still a strategic proposal, not evidence that TRACE-DR would actually achieve those outputs.

## Advisor configuration reasoning

**Historical Stream 01 inference:** the proposed combination could allow a complementary advising structure in which one advisor contributed biomedical NLP/KG/agent expertise and an eligible Health Informatics primary advisor contributed causal, EHR, or real-world-data methodology.

This was a feasibility/fit inference based on public research descriptions, not evidence of willingness or commitment by any faculty member.

## Foundation-model-independence argument

A recurring design principle appeared here for the first time in the available source:

> Prefer contributions that remain meaningful even if the underlying language model becomes substantially more capable.

TRACE-DR was used as an example because causal identification, provenance, database execution, target-trial specification, and auditable tool use were argued to be methodological requirements rather than capabilities guaranteed by a stronger next-generation model.

## Status at the end of this episode

**Hypothesis / untested proposal:** TRACE-DR was an exemplar and candidate research architecture, not a selected dissertation direction.

**Unresolved questions:**

1. Is the proposed combination genuinely one dissertation thesis rather than several adjacent subfields?
2. Which part constitutes the novel methodological object?
3. Which aims could yield publishable results independently if the full end-to-end framework fails?
4. Which components are genuinely generalizable beyond drug repurposing?
5. Which parts would remain scientifically useful after rapid foundation-model improvement?
