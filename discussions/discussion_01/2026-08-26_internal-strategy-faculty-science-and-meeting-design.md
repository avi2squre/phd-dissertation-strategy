# 2026-08-26 — Internal strategy, faculty-facing science, and meeting design

## Historical role

This entry reconstructs the discussion about how dissertation candidates should be presented to faculty and which selection criteria should remain private.

The immediate context was planning a roughly 45-minute exploratory meeting with the research mentor and another collaborator.

## Initial Stream 01 recommendation

Stream 01 first proposed a compact 3–4 page discussion packet rather than a long presentation, with approximately half the meeting reserved for faculty reaction.

A provisional structure was:

- page 1: objective and selection philosophy;
- page 2: 3–4 candidate research programs;
- page 3: comparative matrix;
- page 4: high-value questions and desired outcomes;
- backup literature and detail kept digitally.

The meeting was framed as an uncertainty-reduction exercise rather than an attempt to obtain immediate approval of a final dissertation topic.

## User challenge

The user questioned whether criteria such as labor-market relevance, commercialization, startup potential, citation optimization, and specialized-visa portfolio value should appear in the faculty-facing packet at all.

The user argued that those considerations were useful for choosing which directions to investigate internally, while the faculty discussion should focus on the science.

## Later correction or qualification

Stream 01 agreed and explicitly changed the earlier recommendation.

This produced the project's **two-layer strategy**.

## Layer A — internal strategic selection

The internal decision process may consider a broad objective function, including:

- scientific importance and novelty;
- publication feasibility;
- publication decomposition;
- reusable artifact / adoption potential;
- durable technical skills;
- transferability beyond healthcare;
- labor-market relevance;
- commercialization / translational potential;
- advisor fit and complementarity;
- data / compute / infrastructure feasibility;
- other private strategic considerations.

Detailed private motivations are intentionally not reproduced in this public archive.

## Layer B — faculty-facing scientific assessment

The faculty-facing brief should instead foreground:

- scientific significance;
- unresolved research gaps;
- methodological novelty and depth;
- specific research questions and potential contributions;
- coherence as a multi-study dissertation program;
- feasibility and tractability;
- rigorous evaluation and meaningful baselines;
- failure modes and alternative strategies;
- generalizability;
- available expertise, data, compute, and infrastructure;
- key scientific uncertainties.

It should not foreground private optimization criteria that do not help faculty judge the scientific program.

## Scientifically legitimate proxy questions

Stream 01 identified an important way to preserve useful information without exposing the private objective function.

Instead of asking a faculty member which topic is best for the labor market, ask:

- Which direction requires the deepest methodological training?
- Which skills generalize beyond the immediate healthcare application?

Instead of asking which topic is best for citation accumulation, ask:

- Which direction could generate reusable methods, benchmarks, datasets, or software that other researchers may build upon?

Instead of asking which topic has the best startup potential, ask:

- Which problem represents a consequential unresolved translational bottleneck?

These are legitimate scientific questions whose answers also inform internal strategy.

## Faculty-facing candidate presentation

The proposed packet was designed to present candidate programs as **research programs under investigation**, not as final choices.

A typical candidate row would contain:

- central scientific question;
- core methods;
- healthcare testbed;
- major uncertainty.

The comparative matrix was deliberately simplified. Stream 01 argued against false precision such as 8.37 versus 8.12 at this early stage and recommended leaving some cells explicitly uncertain so faculty could correct them.

## Questions for faculty

Four categories of questions were proposed.

### Scientific

- Which directions contain consequential unsolved problems?
- Which appear novel superficially but are becoming saturated?
- Where are methodological contributions possible rather than mainly application papers?
- Which problems are likely to remain important over several years?

### Execution

- Which directions are supported by available infrastructure, collaborators, and data?
- Which require resources that are not realistically available?
- Which could produce a strong first project relatively early?

### Publication / research-program structure

- Which directions decompose naturally into coherent studies?
- Where is the novelty bar unusually difficult?
- Which methodological contribution types fit the target research communities?

### Advising

- What expertise should the eligible primary advisor contribute that is complementary to the external mentor's expertise?

This was preferred over choosing an advisor name before identifying the missing methodological expertise.

## Desired meeting outputs

Stream 01 proposed that the meeting should ideally reduce uncertainty about:

1. the 2–3 directions worth deeper investigation;
2. directions worth deprioritizing and why;
3. a tractable first research question for the leading area;
4. the complementary primary-advisor expertise required;
5. papers, methods, datasets, researchers, or projects that faculty recommend next.

Selecting the dissertation during that meeting was explicitly **not** required for success.

## Research philosophy clarified

A central framing was:

**generalizable methodological contribution + healthcare as rigorous application/test domain**

rather than:

**healthcare-specific application + minor technical novelty**

The distinction was not meant to devalue domain science. It was intended to protect the dissertation from becoming an application-only project when the user's goal was a technically substantive methodological PhD.

## Repository consequence

The two-layer distinction was then encoded into the repository architecture and a separate portable internal strategy document was introduced for cross-platform use.

### Correction worth preserving

The initial faculty-packet recommendation included too many private strategic criteria. The user's objection materially improved the communication strategy and produced the explicit internal/faculty separation later treated as a project rule.
