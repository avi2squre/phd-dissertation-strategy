## New information that changed the recommendation

**User-stated requirement.** The user clarified that the funded rare-disease project arose from an effort to gain near-term research experience, not from a commitment to make that project the dissertation. The user was open to other biomedical domains and identified prior functional-genomics experience as relevant background.

**User-stated requirement, publicly generalized.** Internal strategic objectives favored methodological work capable of producing independently recognized research contributions, transferable technical skills, external adoption, and strong publication artifacts. A quantitative-career option remained relevant but secondary and should not determine the scientific domain.

## Revision from clinical to biomedical agents

**Later correction or qualification.** The initial formulation centered clinical agents and described the funded project as a planned validation aim. After the clarification, Stream 02 broadened the recommendation to:

> Reliable Biomedical AI Agents: Evaluation, Adaptation, and Selective Control under Temporal and Evidentiary Uncertainty.

The broader question became how a biomedical agent should decide whether to answer, retrieve, use a specialized tool, revise an earlier belief, abstain, or defer when patient information and scientific evidence change.

The proposed contribution set also expanded from benchmark construction alone to three linked forms of work:

1. dynamic measurement of evidence sufficiency, temporal validity, calibration, risk-coverage, and tool reliability;
2. a learned or decision-theoretic action controller;
3. evaluation-guided improvement through post-training, retrieval policy, memory, uncertainty modeling, or verifier training.

**Stream 02 strategic judgment.** A benchmark-only dissertation was considered vulnerable to crowding and rapid model saturation. The controller or adaptation method was expected to provide the more durable methods contribution. This was a prediction, not an empirical result.

## Validation domains and functional genomics

Stream 02 ranked three historical options:

1. general evaluation, adaptation, and selective control of biomedical agents;
2. evidence-grounded scientific agents for functional genomics;
3. longitudinal rare-disease clinical agents through the funded project.

**Stream 02 recommendation.** The dissertation should use one primary domain and, if feasible, one external validation domain rather than attempt several unrelated applications.

The functional-genomics alternative was framed as NLP-centered evidence synthesis and tool use, not an attempt to reproduce protein-structure systems. Proposed functions included reconciling literature and databases; connecting genes, variants, pathways, phenotypes, and experiments; separating causal evidence from association; detecting conflicting or superseded evidence; proposing testable hypotheses; calling analysis tools; and preserving provenance and uncertainty.

**Stream 02 strategic judgment.** This option fit the user's prior experience and could support AI-for-science work, but had greater validation risk. Interesting generated hypotheses would not constitute ground truth. Retrospective time splits, held-out discoveries, expert review, or experimental collaboration would be needed, as would a genuine domain collaborator.

The rare-disease option remained more execution-ready but was judged dissertation-worthy only if it answered a general methods question rather than producing a disease-specific extraction pipeline.

## Adviser-policy correction

**Stream 02 factual claim based on a historical official-source check.** The applicable rule was described as requiring an eligible HINF graduate-faculty adviser with additional advising-experience conditions, not membership in an `IHI core faculty` category. The research mentor was not on the historical HINF graduate-faculty roster, while some people previously dismissed on appointment-type grounds were listed.

**Later correction or qualification.** Roster membership alone was not treated as proof that all advising-experience conditions were satisfied; the program leadership still needed to confirm eligibility. No candidate adviser's willingness was assumed.

Adviser fit was reframed around observable commitments:

- reliable meeting cadence;
- intellectual ownership at the paper level;
- timely manuscript feedback;
- appropriate clinical, methodological, or data access;
- proposal and dissertation advocacy;
- a clear division of responsibility with the research mentor.

Private assessments of named faculty and unconfirmed availability are intentionally omitted.

**Historical inconsistency.** A later fellowship discussion reverted to `IHI-core adviser` shorthand. The archive contains no new policy evidence supporting that reversion, so it is preserved as inconsistent terminology rather than a policy change.

## Research-scientist and research-engineering portfolios

**Stream 02 factual claim based on historical job-description checks.** Research-scientist positions were described as valuing first-author methodological research and the ability to own a sustained agenda. Research-engineering positions were described as additionally demanding production-quality software, scalable evaluation infrastructure, statistics, debugging, and end-to-end systems work.

**Stream 02 recommendation.** The portfolio objective became:

> First-author methodological research plus a technically serious open-source system that other researchers actually use.

Papers with notebook-level code were judged insufficient for research-engineering roles; infrastructure without an independent research contribution was judged weaker for research-scientist and academic paths.

The proposed durable identity was a scientist-engineer who designs, evaluates, and improves high-stakes agents under uncertainty; builds reliable evaluation infrastructure; and understands biomedical evidence well enough to formulate and validate the right problems.

**Later correction or qualification.** The archive rejected the idea of an “AI-immune” dissertation. The aim was instead to develop work whose importance could increase as models became more capable: problem formulation, ground-truth construction, decision theory, statistical reasoning, failure analysis, systems engineering, domain expertise, and responsibility for deployment outcomes.

## Quantitative-career distinction

**Stream 02 strategic judgment.** Frontier AI research and quantitative trading were treated as distinct hiring markets. A biomedical evaluation dissertation could develop calibration, decision-making under uncertainty, distribution-shift analysis, and experimental design, but would not by itself establish readiness for quantitative research.

**Stream 02 recommendation.** Quantitative optionality should be maintained through a separate sidecar involving probability, stochastic processes, time series, optimization, numerical linear algebra, algorithms, systems programming, and work on noisy or nonstationary data. It should not distort the dissertation's scientific question.

## Publication-plan revision

Stream 02 rejected an earlier 6-10-paper baseline with several flagship acceptances, clinical papers, a shared task, and press coverage as too aggressive and too dependent on uncontrollable outcomes.

Two formulations appeared in the same day's discussion:

- a three-paper dissertation core, possibly with an additional position or resource paper and meaningful collaborations;
- a broader aspiration of four to six lead-author outputs, including one or two flagship methods papers, an adopted benchmark or dataset, a reusable system, and a domain-validation paper.

**Qualification.** These are not fully identical targets. The archive does not reconcile them, and neither became an accepted project commitment. Their shared principle was that one independently used contribution could matter more than several thinly sliced papers.
