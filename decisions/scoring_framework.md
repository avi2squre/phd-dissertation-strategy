# Dissertation Program Scoring Framework

Candidate programs must first pass feasibility screening before numerical ranking.

## Stage A — Feasibility filters

A candidate should normally have:

- at least one plausible IHI primary advisor
- meaningful complementarity with Rui Zhang
- plausible data/resources/infrastructure
- PhD-scale methodological depth
- realistic completion feasibility

Candidates failing a major feasibility condition should be flagged before numerical ranking.

## Stage B — Weighted evaluation

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Scientific novelty / importance | 15 |
| Publication feasibility | 15 |
| Publication decomposition / velocity | 12 |
| Citation / artifact-adoption potential | 8 |
| AI-obsolescence resilience | 12 |
| Cross-domain portability | 10 |
| US labor-market demand | 8 |
| Commercialization potential | 8 |
| Specialized-visa portfolio potential | 8 |
| Advisor-team advantage | 4 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Scoring method

Each criterion receives a score from 1–10.

Weighted contribution:

`(score / 10) × criterion weight`

The final score is the sum of weighted contributions.

## Interpretation rule

Advisor availability is primarily a feasibility screen.

Once a strong advising team exists, small differences in advisor fit should not dominate the research ranking unless one team provides a uniquely important advantage such as:

- proprietary data
- unusual methodological expertise
- established infrastructure
- funded project support
- a major collaboration or publication network

## Comparison discipline

- Freeze candidate definitions before scoring.
- Do not add new methodological components to a candidate midway through comparison.
- If a materially different combination is proposed, create it as a separate candidate program.
- Record uncertainty and score ranges when evidence is weak.
- Do not average independent analysis scores mechanically; investigate meaningful disagreements first.
