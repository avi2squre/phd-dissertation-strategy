## Common packet structure

Each packet contains:

1. central scientific object;
2. exact falsifiable thesis;
3. included scientific scope;
4. explicitly excluded scientific scope;
5. constituent Day 1 formulations and relationship;
6. provisional coherent aims;
7. decisive or discriminating experiment;
8. inherited dangerous prior art;
9. known critical uncertainties;
10. feasibility assumptions inherited from Day 1;
11. provenance and change control.

## Drafting rules applied

The coordinator used the following rules:

- preserve the eight identities frozen in `analysis/round3_normalization_reconciliation.md`;
- use the constituent Day 1 v2 artifacts as the primary candidate-definition sources;
- use the Round 2 normalization reviews only to preserve known candidate-definition tensions and boundaries already reconciled in Round 3;
- make no new external literature search;
- preserve inherited source names, identifiers, and prior inspection limits without upgrading them;
- distinguish direct compression from reconciled formulation;
- do not convert a search non-finding into an absence claim;
- do not use Day 1 verdicts as Phase 1 outcomes;
- do not apply publication, career, compensation, immigration, commercialization, prestige, or personal-preference criteria;
- do not silently merge adjacent candidates or create a new candidate;
- mark bibliographic identities that were incomplete in Day 1 as requiring Phase 1 resolution rather than guessing.

## Packet-level fidelity questions

The independent fidelity audits should ask, for every packet:

1. Does the central object faithfully preserve the normalized candidate?
2. Does the exact thesis remain one falsifiable dissertation-level claim?
3. Has any constituent thesis-defining claim been omitted or promoted improperly?
4. Are included and excluded scopes accurate?
5. Are competing mechanisms treated as alternatives rather than cumulative obligations?
6. Are the aims coherent tests of the stated thesis rather than new analysis?
7. Is the decisive experiment a faithful reconciliation of the frozen source material?
8. Does inherited dangerous prior art accurately reflect Day 1 without introducing unsupported bibliographic claims?
9. Are critical uncertainties and feasibility assumptions preserved without converting hypotheses into findings?
10. Would any proposed correction alter candidate identity rather than improve fidelity?

## Independent fidelity-audit workflow

1. Stream 02 and Stream 03 independently inspect this manifest, all eight packets, the Round 3 reconciliation, and the packets' constituent Day 1 v2 sections.
2. Neither stream reads the other's packet-fidelity audit before freezing its own.
3. No external literature search is permitted during packet-fidelity review.
4. Each audit classifies every packet as:
   - `ACCEPT AS DRAFTED`;
   - `ACCEPT WITH EDITORIAL CLARIFICATION`;
   - `REQUIRES MATERIAL FIDELITY CORRECTION`;
   - `CANNOT VERIFY FROM FROZEN SOURCES`.
5. Every proposed correction must identify the source passage or reconciliation rule it preserves.
6. The coordinator reconciles audit disagreements without performing new scientific analysis.
7. The user approves the complete packet set.
8. The approved files receive a final packet-set version, status, updated fingerprints, and canonical integration.
